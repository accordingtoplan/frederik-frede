"Founded in Berlin, 2009. Renamed Friends of Friends eleven years later."

Co-founder and managing director. An interview magazine that became a company: an international network of contributors, books, a production company for brands, two venues in Berlin, events, and the rebrand as Friends of Friends.

Freunde von Freunden started in Berlin in 2009. An online interview magazine that visited creative people at home and at work, in text, photography and film. Co-founded with Tim Seifert and Torsten Bergler. The idea grew out of NoMoreSleep, the agency the founders ran at the time.

Every person portrayed came through the network. A friend, or a friend of a friend. The rule gave the magazine its name and its method.

The magazine became a company. Books, an iPad edition, mixtapes, a production company for brands, an apartment and a loft in Berlin. In 2014 about ten people worked in the Berlin office, with around 150 freelance photographers, filmmakers, producers and editors in the wider network.

01 — The magazine

People, at home and at work.

The core was the interview. Creatives photographed in their homes and studios, then given room to talk. No news, no gossip, no celebrity. Stories meant to stay readable for years. Berlin first, then London, New York, Los Angeles, Paris, Milan, Munich and Tokyo.

02 — Books

The archive, in print.

FvF books gathered the portraits between covers. Published with Distanz, distributed worldwide by Gestalten. Volume one appeared in 2012, volume two in 2015.

03 — Network & channels

A community, kept in conversation.

Readers and contributors met across Facebook, Tumblr, YouTube, Instagram, Twitter and Pinterest. Each channel got its own content instead of one message pushed everywhere. More than 80 mixtapes, compiled by the team and friends, ran as a podcast and on Mixcloud.

04 — Productions for brands

The same eye, on commission.

An in-house production company made films and stories for brands and corporate clients, in the look of FvF. Some ran on the magazine. Others stayed internal. By 2014, partnerships with Vitra, Mercedes-Benz, Design Hotels and the Vienna Business Agency had run for two years or more.

05 — iPad edition

Built for the tablet.

In 2013 the magazine moved onto the iPad as its own product, not a ported website. Concept, design and development in-house at MoreSleep. A built-in video player, touch gestures, portrait and landscape reading, offline downloads. Ten interviews were free. A premium edition added book content, behind-the-scenes material and iPad-only stories. Vitra made it free at launch.

06 — Spaces & events

The magazine, made physical.

After years of portraying people at home, FvF opened a home of its own. The Friends Apartment launched in 2014 in Mulackstraße, Berlin-Mitte. Sixty-five square metres, two bedrooms, a full kitchen, furnished with products from partners. A venue for dinners, workshops, presentations and overnight stays.

Friends Space, Kreuzberg.

In 2016 a larger venue followed. The first floor of a 350 square metre design loft in Kreuzberg, designed with architect Philipp Mainzer. Modular architecture and furniture, a gallery lighting system, an open kitchen built with Siemens, a Sonos surround system. Productions, dinners, talks and brand events, among them a Dr. Hauschka launch event.

The team also took events offsite. Talks for MINI across Germany, the Sonos press hub at IFA, dinners for CLOSED, &Tradition in Milan, and evenings with Reform, Ace & Tate, Odeeh and AWAY. 2017 to 2019.

07 — Friends of Friends

A new name for an international readership.

After eleven years and almost 700 interviews, the platform relaunched as Friends of Friends. The German name had become hard to pronounce for most readers. A plain translation kept the meaning. A new identity followed: logo animation, colour, typography and motion, built to travel across editorial and social. Typefaces: Adieu by Good Type Foundry and Untitled Sans by Klim Type Foundry.

A new home for 700 stories.

The website was rebuilt from the ground up. A modular system let editors compose articles with unusual layouts. New interactions and a deeper search opened the full archive.

A place for sharing stories. Accessible to everybody.

An identity that moves with the stories.

Logo animation, social templates, showreel, merchandise and print. Each channel received its own content.
