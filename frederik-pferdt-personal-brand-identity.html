"Radical optimism as a personal brand."

Personal brand strategy and identity for Google's first Chief Innovation Evangelist: transitioning from corporate leader to independent innovator, author, and keynote speaker.

For twelve years Frederik Pferdt was Google's first Chief Innovation Evangelist. That title opens every door and belongs to someone else. Going independent as a speaker and author meant the reputation had to detach from the logo and stand on his own name, with nothing borrowed to lean on.

The work built that name from the strategy out. What he actually stands for, radical optimism, turned into a brand he owns rather than a role he held. The scope ran the full stage: identity, keynote system, website, and social templates, built so the next chapter reads as a person, not a former job title.

01 — Brand Strategy & Identity

A mark for a mind that builds the future.

Working closely with illustrator Stefan Schuster, the identity came together as a bespoke system: logo, colour, and typography reflecting Frederik's innovative spirit and professional gravitas, held consistent across business cards, stationery, and all print collateral.

02 — Keynote & Video

Built for 10,000-seat auditoriums.

Guidelines for keynotes and video content hold a coherent, engaging communication style and a consistent narrative across diverse global audiences.

03 — Digital

A platform for radical optimism.

The personal branding conveys a futuristic look built on morphing shapes, a minimalistic disrupted layout, and a subtle colour palette. A smooth scrolling experience connects all sections, using the shapes as a recurring storytelling element that ties the full site together.

04 — Social Media

Thought leadership beyond Google.

The social strategy on Instagram and LinkedIn blends professional insight with personal anecdote, building a deeper connection with his audience and establishing Frederik as a global voice in innovation.

From Google's Innovation Lab to an independent force in innovation.

Frederik G. Pferdt is Google's first Chief Innovation Evangelist, the founder of Google's Innovation Lab and the person who trained tens of thousands of Googlers in creative thinking. After two decades at Google, he was transitioning to an independent practice as a keynote speaker, author, and innovation advisor.

The personal brand was built to carry his reputation into this new chapter, capturing his radical optimism in a distinctive visual identity, keynote system, website, and social presence that position him as a global voice in innovation.
